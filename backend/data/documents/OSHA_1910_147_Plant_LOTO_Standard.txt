# OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 — CONTROL OF HAZARDOUS ENERGY (LOCKOUT/TAGOUT) MASTER STANDARD
Document Code: STD-OSHA-1910-147-2026
Standard Title: General Industry Standard for the Control of Hazardous Energy Sources
Regulatory Authority: Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA), U.S. Department of Labor
Compliance Status: MANDATORY PLANT-WIDE REGULATION (Zero Tolerance Policy)
Effective Date: January 1, 2026 | Revision: 4.0

SECTION 1: PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This master safety standard establishes mandatory minimum requirements for the control of hazardous energy (electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, chemical, and thermal) during servicing, maintenance, and unjamming of industrial machinery.

Scope of Application:
1. Applies to all machining centers (CNC-01, CNC-02, CNC-04), hydraulic stamping presses (PRESS-01, PRESS-02), robotic welding cells (ROBOT-01, ROBOT-02), and air compressors (COMP-01).
2. Applies whenever an employee is required to remove or bypass a machine guard or other safety device.
3. Applies whenever an employee is required to place any part of their body into an area on a machine or piece of equipment where work is actually performed upon the material being processed (point of operation).

⚠️ DANGER: LIFE SAFETY HAZARD
Unexpected energization, startup of machines, or release of stored energy during servicing will cause catastrophic personal injury, severe arc flash burns, limb amputation, or death.

SECTION 2: ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

1. Authorized Employee (Certified Maintenance Technicians & Electricians):
   - Only individuals who have completed OSHA 1910.147 Authorized certification and hold an active plant lock/key assignment.
   - Authorized to apply padlocks, danger tags, and perform zero-energy state verification.

2. Affected Employee (Machine Operators & Floor Laborers):
   - Employees whose job requires them to operate or use a machine or equipment on which servicing or maintenance is being performed under lockout or tagout.
   - MUST NEVER attempt to bypass, remove, tamper with, or operate equipment under Lockout/Tagout.

3. Other Employees (Supervisors, Visitors, Support Personnel):
   - All employees who work in an area where energy control procedures are utilized. Must be instructed about the prohibition relating to attempts to restart or reenergize locked out machines.

SECTION 3: MANDATORY 7-STEP ZERO-ENERGY ISOLATION PROTOCOL

Every authorized technician must strictly execute the following sequential 7-step energy control procedure prior to servicing:

Step 1: Preparation for Shutdown & Mandatory Notification
- Notify all affected operators, floor laborers, and shift supervisors that a lockout/tagout procedure is being implemented.
- Review specific machine energy control tag: identify all electrical disconnects, pneumatic valves, hydraulic accumulators, and gravity counterweights.

Step 2: Orderly Equipment Shutdown
- Shut down the machine using standard stopping procedures (normal stop pushbutton or HMI power cycle).
- Ensure all moving axes, chucks, spindles, and robotic arms come to a complete mechanical standstill.

Step 3: Machine Energy Isolation
- Operate all energy-isolating devices to completely disconnect the machine from energy sources:
  a. Open main electrical disconnect switches or circuit breakers.
  b. Close manual pneumatic isolation ball valves.
  c. Close hydraulic supply shut-off valves.

Step 4: Application of Lockout/Tagout Devices
- Secure each energy-isolating device in the "OFF" or "SAFE" position with an OSHA-approved standardized RED safety padlock.
- Affix a standardized DANGER tag displaying: "DO NOT OPERATE", Technician Name, Employee ID, Date, and Contact Extension.
- For group lockout involving multiple technicians, each technician MUST attach their own personal padlock to an approved lockout hasp or lockbox.

Step 5: Release of Stored Hazardous Residual Energy
- ⚠️ CRITICAL: Relieve or mechanically restrain all stored residual energy:
  a. Capacitors & DC-Bus: Allow discharge time (minimum 10 minutes) and verify zero voltage.
  b. Hydraulic Accumulators: Open manual dump valve to discharge pressure to 0 Bar.
  c. Pneumatics: Depress secondary manual dump valve until manifold gauge registers 0 PSI.
  d. Gravity/Kinetic Energy: Mechanically block elevated machine rams or vertical carriages with safety drop stops.

Step 6: Zero-Energy State Verification (The "Test Before Touch" Rule)
- Prior to starting any physical work, the authorized technician must verify isolation:
  a. Multimeter Voltage Check: Test incoming phase-to-phase and phase-to-ground (L1, L2, L3) with a calibrated CAT IV 600V digital multimeter. Reading must be < 1.0 VAC/VDC.
  b. Pressure Gauge Inspection: Confirm zero pressure on all inline analog and digital pressure gauges.

Step 7: Verification by Restart Attempt
- Ensure operating personnel are clear of the machinery boundary.
- Attempt to start the machine using the operator HMI or start pushbutton.
- Verify that the machine remains completely unpowered and does not actuate.
- Return operating control switches to the "OFF" position after the restart attempt.

SECTION 4: RELEASE FROM LOCKOUT / TAGOUT (RESTORATION PROCEDURE)

Before lockout or tagout devices are removed and energy is restored, the following procedures MUST be followed:
1. Machine Inspection: Inspect the work area to ensure that nonessential items (tools, spare parts, rags) have been removed and machine components are mechanically intact.
2. Guard Replacement: Reinstall and secure all fixed interlocks, covers, and safety guards.
3. Employee Clearance: Check the work area to ensure that all employees have been safely positioned or cleared from the machine boundary.
4. Notification: Notify affected employees that the lockout or tagout devices are being removed.
5. Lock Removal: Each lockout device MUST be removed from each energy isolating device by the employee who applied the device.
